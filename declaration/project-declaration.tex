\textbf{Description of the Co-Designer}
\begin{body}
Manuel is an 80-year-old widower and retired electrician who lives alone in Lisbon. He plays bisca with a friend at the caf\'e every day and has Sunday lunch at his daughter's. He calls his family every day on a button phone he has used for years.

In July, his daughter bought him a smartphone so he could join the family's WhatsApp group, where the family shares its news and photos of his newborn great-grandson. Manuel does not know how to work the smartphone, so it stays in a drawer, and his daughter reads the group's news to him over the phone. He does not want a simpler phone: he wants what the smartphone holds, without having to learn how to work it.
\end{body}

\vspace{6mm}
\textbf{Problem statement}
\begin{body}
Manuel needs a way to see the new photos of his great-grandson, to hear his great-granddaughter and the family group, and to reply with a short sentence, without learning the smartphone. The group is where the baby is shown and where news is written. He needs the sender's name first, because a message that says \textquotedblleft ol\'a pai\textquotedblright{} (\textquotedblleft hi dad\textquotedblright) may be a scam.
\end{body}
